# SPOB high-divergence calibration set

This set prevents SPOB from being calibrated only on easy or familiar verses.
Each passage has a documented lexical, syntactic, textual, or interpretive choice
that English translations commonly expose in different ways.

The list combines source-aware editorial knowledge with the automated wording
divergence indicator. The score locates candidates; source text and POB's audit
trail adjudicate them.

## Governing-image passages

### Ecclesiastes 1:2 — *hevel*

Calibrate whether SPOB communicates vapor-like transience, elusiveness, and ironic
mismatch without importing the stronger claim that nothing matters. “Vapor” is
preferable to scientific “gas” in the main text because it evokes something
briefly visible that cannot be held.

### Genesis 1:1

Calibrate the relation between the opening creation statement and the following
description. SPOB should make the sentence followable without pretending the
Hebrew syntax has only one possible analysis.

## Genesis pilot anchors

- **Genesis 4:7** — acceptance/lifted-up language; sin's crouching image; its
  “desire”; the command to rule over it.
- **Genesis 16:13** — Hagar's divine name and the difficult statement about seeing
  the One who sees her or surviving that sight.
- **Genesis 32:25–33 in POB versification** — the identity of Jacob's opponent,
  “striving with God,” injury language, Peniel, and a source-oriented verse offset
  from common English Bibles.
- **Genesis 49:6** — “hamstrung an ox” versus a materially different traditional
  rendering; a useful textual-tradition flag.

## Luke pilot anchors

- **Luke 1:37** — whether the saying is framed as nothing being impossible for God,
  no divine word lacking power, or no divine word failing.
- **Luke 7:23** — stumbling, falling away, or taking offense because of Jesus.
- **Luke 8:36** — the semantic overlap of being saved, healed, or made whole.
- **Luke 9:51** — being “taken up” and Jesus setting his face toward Jerusalem.
- **Luke 16:9** — unrighteous wealth, who fails, and who welcomes whom into eternal
  dwellings.
- **Luke 17:21** — God's kingdom “within,” “among,” or “in the midst of” the
  audience; context must prevent an exclusively private inward reading.
- **Luke 17:37** — vultures/eagles and body/carcass in the closing image.

## Canon-wide future anchors

- **Psalm 22:16** — difficult Hebrew wording represented by “like a lion” and
  “pierced” traditions.
- **Isaiah 7:14** — immediate historical wording and later “young woman/virgin”
  reception.
- **John 1:1** — the relation expressed in the final clause about the Word and God.
- **Romans 3:25** — mercy-seat/atoning-place imagery and later atonement terms.
- **1 Corinthians 6:9** — disputed vice-list terms that should not be collapsed
  into modern categories without lexical evidence.
- **Philippians 2:6** — grasping, exploiting, or treating equality as an advantage.
- **1 Timothy 2:12** — the rare authority verb and the scope of the prohibition.
- **Revelation 13:18** — textual evidence for the number and how uncertainty is
  disclosed.

## Passing standard

For every anchor, reviewers should be able to identify:

1. what ordinary readers are likely to misunderstand;
2. what the source and immediate context establish;
3. what remains genuinely disputed;
4. what SPOB makes explicit in the main text;
5. what alternative is preserved in a note; and
6. whether POB → SPOB continuity remains visible in the divergence index.
