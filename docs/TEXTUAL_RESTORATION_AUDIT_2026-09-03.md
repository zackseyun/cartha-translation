# Canonical textual-restoration audit — 2026-09-03

Updated through 2026-09-04. The
[approach review](TEXTUAL_RESTORATION_APPROACH_REVIEW_2026-09-04.md) and
[method 2.0](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md) now govern calibration, review,
and execution order. The case findings below retain their working status.

## Outcome

POB has a credible source-comparison foundation, but it does not yet have a
complete reconstructed Old or New Testament critical text. The existing
corpus-wide inventory is a screening layer. It must not be described as though
all surviving manuscripts have already been collated or as though the earliest
attainable wording has been established at every verse.

The right target remains the **earliest attainable text supported by surviving
evidence**, with uncertainty preserved. “Optimal translation” has two distinct
operations:

1. choose or delimit the source wording through textual criticism; and
2. translate that wording through source-near lexical, syntactic, discourse,
   and literary analysis.

Neither operation can be replaced by counting witnesses, preferring the oldest
copy automatically, or selecting the English sentence that reads most smoothly.

## Current coverage

The reproducible inventory currently reports:

- 31,221 canonical verse files across all 66 books;
- 590 local POB footnote leads;
- 1,260 WLC qere/ketiv records;
- 1,072 WLC editorial or transcription annotations; and
- 6,934 SBLGNT edition-apparatus entries at 4,468 New Testament anchors.

These categories overlap. The Hebrew inventory is not yet a full
MT–Judean Desert–Samaritan–Old Greek collation, and the New Testament inventory
compares edited Greek texts rather than supplying a complete manuscript-level
ECM/CBGM apparatus. The 48-case casebook is therefore the present
meaning-changing adjudication queue, not a claim that only 48 variants matter.

## Audit findings

### Genesis 4:8

The WLC/MT text has an introductory “Cain said to Abel his brother” without
recorded speech. Direct Hebrew witness 4QGenb (4Q2) likewise moves immediately
into the field scene. The Old Greek has the invitation *dielthōmen eis to
pedion*, and other ancient witnesses preserve a comparable longer form.

Before this audit, the POB main text followed the shorter WLC form and footnoted
the longer reading, while its lexical and theological metadata incorrectly said
the longer clause had been included in the main text. The metadata now describes
the actual text. This is an audit-trail repair, not an adjudication of which form
is earliest.

Next evidence gate: verify fragment 3 i against the IAA color and infrared
images, consult the DJD material notes, classify the daughter-version evidence,
and then make an explicit adoption or non-adoption decision.

### Exodus 1:5

POB presently follows the Masoretic count of seventy. Re-audit on 2026-09-04
corrected an overclaim: 4Q13 preserves seventy-five, 4Q1 preserves “and five”
after supplied “seventy,” and 4Q11's entire numeral is supplied in a gap.
4Q11 is not preserved support for seventy. The Old Greek count of seventy-five must
still be evaluated together with Genesis 46:20/27 and Acts 7:14. A New Testament
quotation is reception evidence, not another Hebrew manuscript.

Next evidence gate: verify the three fragments against institutional images,
separate the count from the Joseph-clause variation, and distinguish whether
seventy-five reflects a different genealogical reckoning, harmonization, or an
earlier form.

### Exodus 12:40

POB follows the shorter Masoretic geographical wording, “in Egypt.” The
2026-09-04 re-audit corrected the earlier claim of two Egypt-only witnesses:
2Q2's geography is supplied; 4Q14 preserves Egypt followed by the duration,
but its missing beginning cannot exclude every longer form. See the corrected
[pass 2 evidence boundaries](PENTATEUCH_SOURCE_COMPARISON_PASS_2.md).
The longer forms in the Old Greek and Samaritan traditions change the
chronology’s scope, but shared expansion can also explain the agreement.
Chronological harmonization is not in itself proof of originality.

This audit aligned the lexical metadata with the actual phrase “who lived.” It
did not choose between the shorter and longer textual forms.

Next evidence gate: verify the 2Q2 and 4Q14 fragments against institutional
images, consult their DJD editions, and then assess whether the two distinct
longer forms or the Masoretic shorter form best explains the others, without
turning supplied wording into visible attestation.

### Deuteronomy 27:4

The Masoretic and Old Greek controls read Mount Ebal; the Samaritan Pentateuch
reads Mount Gerizim. 4QDeutf (4Q33) covers the verse, but the whole mountain-name
phrase is inside an editorially reconstructed lacuna. It therefore provides no
direct support for either reading. The coverage and comparison schemas now
encode that distinction rather than leaving it to prose.

Next evidence gate: verify the exact fragment placement and imagery, consult
DJD XIV, and adjudicate only authenticated and securely provenanced evidence.

### Deuteronomy 32:43

4QDeutq (4Q44) preserves a longer Hebrew form containing the rejoicing heavens,
the worship of all gods, “his sons,” repayment of those who hate him, and “the
land of his people.” The Old Greek is related but longer still; the Masoretic
and Samaritan controls are shorter. These must be compared as distinct forms,
not collapsed into a single majority count.

The fragment is now located at plate level on IAA image B-280818 / PAM
M42.164. No image has been vendored and no measured region or independent
diplomatic transcription is claimed.

Next evidence gate: verify the plate and visible letters, align the individual
variation units, and run the full external-and-internal adjudication before
proposing a longer English main text.

### Deuteronomy 32:8

The versioned 4QDeutj (4Q37) transcription directly reads `בני אלוהים`, “sons
of God.” The Old Greek control reads “angels of God,” while the Masoretic and
Samaritan controls read “sons of Israel.” 4QpaleoDeutr (4Q45) overlaps the verse
but loses the decisive final phrase and is now explicitly classified as
coverage-only.

The existing moderate working preference for a divine referent is strengthened.
The main English has not yet changed because its verse record still declares
WLC as the canonical source; source-text selection and English must be promoted
together rather than allowed to diverge.

A validated non-promoted selection bundle now enforces that rule. It ties the
working `בני אלוהים` variation unit and “the sons of God” rendering to the same
comparison, adjudication, canonical baseline hash, retained readings, and review
gates. It cannot advance while the full-verse critical Hebrew source or any
required review remains pending.

Next evidence gate: crosswalk DJD fragment 12 to the IAA image identifier,
verify the visible phrase in color and infrared, collate the early Greek “sons”
witnesses, and complete the full-verse source side of the atomic bundle.

### 1 Samuel 17:4

The 4QSama (4Q51) evidence has now been upgraded from a secondary report to a
versioned manuscript transcription. Fragments 12–14, line 3 preserves the
decisive numeral as `]א֯רבע[`, with the alef marked uncertain and the remaining
letters supporting four. The surrounding reconstruction and partly supplied
word for “cubits” are not treated as visible ink. The pinned Rahlfs control also
reads four cubits and a span; WLC reads six.

The POB main wording remains synchronized to WLC, but its footnote marker and
disclosure have been repaired so readers now see the four/six variant and both
approximate conversions. The prior moderate working preference for four is
stronger, not final.

Next evidence gate: locate the exact IAA image region, verify the numeral,
consult the DJD XVII material notes, collate individual Greek witnesses, and
evaluate the number within the larger short/long Samuel literary forms before
creating an atomic promotion bundle.

## Operational evidence gates

The Old Testament source-discovery ledger is
[`../sources/textual_restoration/ot_witness_registry.v1.json`](../sources/textual_restoration/ot_witness_registry.v1.json).
Its schema prevents a discovery record from silently becoming textual evidence.
Every passage must clear these gates in order:

| Gate | Required evidence | Output | May affect English? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Stable institutional or edition access point, shelfmark, rights state, language, date basis | Registry record | No |
| 2. Coverage | Exact book/passage survival, page or fragment, and positive/no-coverage distinction | Passage coverage map | No |
| 3. Pixels or published text | Archival-image hash and region, or an explicitly labeled published reading | Immutable evidence object | No |
| 4. Transcription | Diplomatic text, hands/corrections, gaps, supplied characters, independent model-pass provenance where pixels are read | Witness transcription | No |
| 5. Normalization and alignment | Normalized tokens mapped back to diplomatic tokens and POB references | Variation unit | No |
| 6. Text selection | Relationship groups, competing transmission explanations, chronological effect, counterargument, calibrated decision | Source-wording decision | Not yet |
| 7. English impact | Literal effect, discourse/context effect, natural-English draft, note requirement, and baseline hash | Proposed POB change or no-change record | Proposed only |
| 8. Promotion | Required review complete; source, decision, verse, notes, and exports synchronized | Canonical commit | Yes |

Failure at a gate is preserved as `unmapped`, `no-coverage`, `lost`,
`hypothesis-only`, or another explicit non-promoted state. A missing witness is
not a vote for either reading.

## Codex vision and ImageGen

Codex vision may inspect archival pixels and deterministic derivatives. When
the same registered region is read independently, the repository stores the
actual passes and reconciles visible and supplied characters separately.

ImageGen may create an educational, complete-looking reconstruction only after
the textual decision exists. It must be watermarked **RECONSTRUCTED — NOT
MANUSCRIPT EVIDENCE**, stored outside the evidence directories, and excluded
from OCR, collation, confidence, and translation inputs. It therefore cannot
restore the source corpus in the evidentiary sense. Its useful role is to show
readers what an already documented manuscript line or page may have looked
like.

## Publication language

Until each target clears its evidence gate, describe the work as
**machine-restored and source-compared**, or as **published-witness
adjudication** where no archival image was re-read. Do not call a reading
“original,” “recovered,” or “definitive” merely because two models agree or an
older witness supports it.
