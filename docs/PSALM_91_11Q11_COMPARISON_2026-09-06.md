# Psalm 91 in 11Q11: text, restoration, and reception

Date: 2026-09-06. Agent research; no human or specialist sign-off. Companion record: `sources/textual_restoration/discovery/psalm91_11q11_comparison.v1.json`.

## Result

The catalogue lead now reaches an actual published Hebrew transcription, not just an index hit. The accessible published preview reports consequential differences at Psalm 91:2, 4, and 6, and a substantially different but damaged verse 9. **Verse 13 and the compressed ending were consulted in private QDR only, beyond that preview's coverage**, so they remain provisional until edition/image confirmation. These justify a witness-specific comparison; they do **not** establish that its collection form should replace the WLC-based POB Psalm.

No reading, translation, image coordinate, or canonical change is accepted here. In particular, **do not restore “you have said/called” to POB 91:9 as something fully preserved in 11Q11**. The initial two letters of the proposed Hebrew speech verb are editorially supplied. The current POB already discloses the Masoretic syntactic ambiguity and has no speech verb in its main text.

## Contrary explanations and adjudication limit

The strongest case **for** adding a speech verb at verse 9 is not merely “Qumran is older.” Švarc argues that an elided speech introduction can connect verse 9 to verse 2, with the Qumran reconstruction suggesting the particular verb. But he also describes the current POB-type single-verb construal as syntactically possible (p. 12). His pp. 14–16 report incompatible reconstructions of the damaged Qumran sentence: proposed “made” versus “were,” and disputed final suffix. His report of Pajunen's contrary case favors MT's poetic structure over a shorter Qumran recasting; this is **Pajunen as reported by Švarc**, not a claim to have read Pajunen's full article. The alternative is therefore a serious interpretive proposal, not an ink-verified correction forced on POB.

The strongest contrary explanation for transferring 11Q11's readings wholesale is **adaptation for this collection's use**: changed address, expansions, sequence and ending could represent a performed protective form rather than an earlier form of every affected verse. Conversely, different collection use alone cannot invalidate individual old readings, especially the partial Greek agreements. This check does not establish direction of dependence.

Guerra reports both exorcistic classification and an oracle-of-salvation challenge, cautions that the total number of compositions is uncertain, and treats the closing response as partly reconstructed. She also leaves prophylactic versus threat-specific use unresolved. Thus demons in neighboring compositions, or a reconstructed congregational response, are evidence about reception/function—not automatic new nouns or deleted promises in Psalm 91. The internal verse-6 transposition must also be distinguished from the order of whole compositions.
