## Competing explanations, without theological shortcuts

**Retain the Masoretic expression:** it is the actual declared source, with
animal imagery in the surrounding psalm. However, retention does not solve
the awkward relationship to the hands and feet, explain the alternative
Hebrew spelling, or establish that this is the earliest recoverable wording.
Original-language primacy cannot automatically prefer one Hebrew witness
over another.

**A verbal reading interpreted as digging/piercing:** the Greek supplies a
verb and the Nahal Hever transcription supplies a serious alternative Hebrew
form. However, the aleph in כארו must be explained rather than silently removed
to produce כרו; identifying a possible third-person plural ending does not
settle the root or its meaning. “Pierced” is not a translation of WLC כארי.
The relationship between Greek and Hebrew evidence requires analysis, not
counting agreement as two votes for an exact reconstructed Hebrew sentence.

**Other proposed verbal readings and resegmentation:** these remain in the
research queue, not silently excluded because they fall outside the familiar
two-option debate. Flowers surveys thirteen proposals; the abstract was
consulted, but the full article was not accessible in this pass. Do not report
its detailed conclusions as verified here.
[Flowers, Vetus Testamentum 71 (2021), 48-75, online 2020](https://doi.org/10.1163/15685330-12341438).

Chronology does not resolve the lexical problem. No numerical confidence or
claim about an individual scribe's religious motive is justified by this pass.
Neither usefulness for a Christian interpretation nor avoidance of one can
select the original reading.

## Checking the scholarly arguments, not just collecting them

Hopkin argues for “pierced” and reports the DJD editors' confidence in the
Nahal Hever reading, while discussing Swenson's objection to legibility.
Those reports identify the need for direct DJD/image review; they are not our
own successful decipherment. Swenson's full paper was not retrieved.
[Hopkin, BYU Studies 44.3 (2005), 161-172](https://byustudies.byu.edu/article/the-psalm-22-16-controversy-new-evidence-from-the-dead-sea-scrolls),
[Swenson, JBL 123.4 (2004), 637-648](https://doi.org/10.2307/3268463).

One checkable weakness in Hopkin's argument matters methodologically: he says
the Greek uses the same verb in the ears passage, Hebrew Psalm 40:7, as in
Psalm 22. Our pinned Rahlfs control at Greek Ps 39:7 instead has **κατηρτίσω**,
not **ὤρυξαν**. This does not erase the possible Hebrew lexical parallel; it
means the claimed identical Greek verb is not supported by the control we
checked. A full Greek apparatus could assess other readings. Do not copy a
source's ancillary argument into POB merely because its main conclusion seems
plausible. Both Greek checks use the same pinned file/hash recorded in the
[comparison source receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/comparisons/psalms_controls.v1.json).

## Versional follow-up: Syriac and Aramaic checked directly

Checked 2026-09-04. Two edition-derived controls now join the same case; they
are not two newly collated manuscripts or two new comparison cases. Both CAL
chapter displays locate this clause at **022:017**, aligned here with POB 22:16.

| Control | Short consulted excerpt | Our working gloss and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Peshitta, CAL 62027 | ܒܙܥܘ ܐ̈ܝܕܝ ܘܪ̈ܓܠܝ | “They tore/pierced my hands and feet”: an injury action, not a uniquely recoverable Hebrew verb |
| Targum Psalms, CAL 81002 | נכתין היך כאריא אידי ורגלי | “Biting like a lion my hands and feet”: preserves lion imagery with an explicit biting action, not a verbatim copy of terse Masoretic syntax |

[Targum Psalms](https://cal.huc.edu/get_a_chapter.php?cset=H&file=81002&sub=022).

a Hebrew manuscript or a work attributed here to Jonathan.

CAL tags the Syriac verb as **D**. Its range includes tearing and penetration;
do not justify “pierced” solely by selecting a gloss under the G stem. The
Targum's tokens are identified as a biting verb and a lion comparison.

**Inference and strongest counterarguments:** the Syriac strengthens the case
that an injury interpretation belongs in the dossier, but its wording could
reflect interpretation or influence from Greek rather than independently
preserved Hebrew כארו or כרו. The Targum confirms a lion interpretation, but
its added action could be explanatory repair rather than evidence that the
Masoretic syntax is original. Even though both versions express an action,
they do not thereby attest the same source verb. Their local transmission
relationships and priority remain unestablished in this pass.

Neither short excerpt displays a local variant marker; variants appear
elsewhere in the chapter/verse. That is not a complete apparatus or proof of
agreement among all Syriac or Aramaic copies. CAL's AI-assistance links were
not used as evidence. Only brief cited excerpts are retained, not a licensed
corpus import or a frozen full-page snapshot.
