## What was acquired and seen

The IAA records identify [B-472934](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-472934)
as full-spectrum color and [B-472935](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-472935)
as infrared: both recto, plate265 fragment24, May2014, Shai Halevi, linked to
4Q122 / 4Q LXXDeut. Actual institutional HTML was read for both; web rendering
of the second failed but ordinary HTTPS returned its complete metadata page.
The image-service URLs came from those pages' image-viewer attributes.

Both downloaded images are **1200×1200 pixels**, inspected in full at native
display detail. The public `=s0` request returned that size; it is not proof of
an original camera file. The color viewer's `=g` tile metadata reports a
**3296×3296** pyramid, which was not acquired or stitched. The first unmodified
base-URL response was only512×512. Received dimensions, not a URL parameter's
name or advertised metadata, control the resolution claim.

[Robert Kraft's scholarly working survey, section01](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/earlylxx/jewishpap.html#01)
links a [294×226 image](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rs/rak/lxx4qdeut.jpg), also
downloaded and inspected. Its outline, central hole, notch below the second
writing line, lower taper and surviving writing layout visibly match the
larger IAA target despite contrast, scale and backing differences. That is a
contextual image-correspondence check, not a registered geometric transform or
an independent witness. IAA fragment24 is not thereby equated with a DJD
fragment number; the actual DJD plate/numbering remains unconsulted.

No crop, sharpening, thresholding, rotation, upscaling, inpainting or ImageGen
was used on these manuscript images. Two exposures and a scholarly thumbnail
represent the same apparent fragment, not three textual votes. The original
spectral measurements and processing history were not obtained.

## Reading boundary and identification

The second visible writing line fits the published **ERUQRAS** / `ἐρυθρᾶς`
(“red”) sequence in context. The region around theta and the second rho is
damaged beside the central hole; it must not be described as seven pristine,
independently transcribed letters. This is a qualitative, report-aware fit
with a published reading, not a frozen image-only transcription or new
decipherment. An independent agent's preliminary view of both exposures and
the thumbnail agrees with that limited description; its formal final-artifact
review is separate.

Kraft attributes the relevant identification and paleographical comments to
Ulrich and Parsons in DJD IX. His quoted account explicitly cautions that very
little complete wording survives and that the red-word identification is
fragile. His report of a second-century-BCE dating also preserves unusually
large uncertainty because the sample is small. We consulted Kraft's actual
section, **not** DJD IX pp.195–197 or Parsons pp.11–12. Treat the customary
Deuteronomy11:4 alignment as a published working identification, not a newly
demonstrated unique identification from these images. No complete-column
layout or letter-per-line reconstruction was tested here.

The adjacent visible short sequences are compatible with the conventional
alignment, but no full verse was supplied or accepted. In particular, the
divine-name slot of the aligned complete verse is not preserved here. A
completed modern Greek verse containing *kyrios* cannot be attributed to
surviving 4Q122 ink. Nor are the other small fragments associated with this
catalogue label collated by inspecting this one IAA fragment.

## Comparison with current POB

The [Rahlfs–Hanhart publisher text at Deuteronomy11:4](https://www.die-bibel.de/bibel/LXX/DEU.11)
contains `τῆς θαλάσσης τῆς ἐρυθρᾶς`. This directly consulted selected Greek
edition is a comparison control, not the full Göttingen apparatus or a claim
that 4Q122 preserves that whole phrase. Its red-word form is compatible with
the qualified fragment observation. The rest of the selected verse is not
converted into fragment evidence.

Current `translation/ot/deuteronomy/011/004.yaml` has Hebrew `יַם סוּף`,
English **Red Sea**, and a note explaining **Sea of Reeds**. The Greek
translation's adjective does not establish a Hebrew word for “red,” decide
the location of the crossing, or settle POB's source-near English policy.
Evidence of an ancient Greek rendering is distinct from a Hebrew textual
variant or a geographic identification. No replacement Hebrew is proposed.
The old HALOT-labelled rationale was read as historical metadata; HALOT was
not freshly consulted.

There is a separate, directly observable editorial defect: the note marker
currently follows **Egypt**, although its note concerns **Red Sea**. The
appropriate next note-placement candidate would attach it to the sea phrase
without changing the source or using this fragment to settle the English
term. No exact canonical candidate, metadata reset or export was applied in
this pass. Its historical agreement score is not approval of a future repair.
