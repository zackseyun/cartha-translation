# Three UXLC orthographic leads: actual Leningrad image follow-up

Date: 2026-09-06. Scope: 2 Samuel 14:7; 2 Chronicles 27:4; Amos 7:2. Research only; no canonical, source-label, English, or note changes. Companion: `sources/textual_restoration/discovery/uxlc_orthographic_image_followup.v1.json`.

## Outcome and review boundary

All three requested **full color folios were legitimately accessible**, downloaded privately, and visually inspected with native-resolution context and word crops. Folio numbers, institutional page mappings, and neighboring Hebrew identify the loci. The observed marks **support the UXLC published spellings**: an additional yod in each of Samuel and Chronicles, and no vav between kaf and final lamed in Amos. Samuel's pale/irregular stroke warrants more caution than the clearer Chronicles and Amos contrasts.

This is **one context-informed agent observation**, after exposure to both spellings and publisher explanations. It is not a blinded transcription, independent two-family agreement, human/specialist review, or completed calibration. The mandatory [adjudication method](TEXTUAL_ADJUDICATION_METHOD.md) keeps fresh transcription acceptance and canonical promotion separate; those gates remain open. No generated letters, ImageGen, spectral reconstruction, contrast enhancement, interpolation, or restoration was used.

The newly advanced evidence is actual pixels and image/locus identity. The five-lead digital screen and publisher change explanations were already established in [the prior report](UXLC_WLC_WHOLE_OT_SCREEN_2026-09-06.md); they are not counted as new manuscript discoveries or extra witnesses.

## Observation, proposed transcription, and English consequence

### 2 Samuel 14:7 — yod between mem and tav

The [publisher's entry `2022.08.30-24`](https://www.tanach.us/Changes/2022.12.07%20-%20Changes/2022.12.07%20-%20Changes.html#2022.08.30-24) changes `ונמתהו` to `ונמיתהו`, explains the pale lower part as possible flaking, and reports disagreement between Dotan's BHL text and its uncertain Appendix-A proposal. **BHL itself was not consulted.**

Own observation in [folio 176A](https://manuscripts.sefaria.org/leningrad-color/BIB_LENCDX_F176A.jpg): the native context includes `המשפחה על שפחתך ויאמרו` before the target line, `תני את מכה אחיו` on it, and `בנפש אחיו אשר הרג` after it. Within the target word there is a separate small dark head/stroke at the consonantal writing height between the mem and tav; its lower portion is paler/irregular. It is not simply the below-line hiriq. This favors reading the extra yod, but **flaking is a publisher explanation, not a physical cause proved by this photograph**. Damage, a nonletter ink trace, and contextual expectation are the strongest contrary risks. A second blinded assessment is still required for fresh-reading acceptance.

Current POB expresses putting him to death. The plene/defective spelling contrast does not change that action, person, or object. The prior screen's extra terminal paragraph-marker mismatch in the canonical source field is a separate encoding issue, not a third spelling of this word; nothing here repairs it.

### 2 Chronicles 27:4 — additional yod after resh

The [publisher's entry `2023.08.10-10`](https://www.tanach.us/Changes/2023.10.19%20-%20Changes/2023.10.19%20-%20Changes.html#2023.08.10-10) changes `בירניות` to `ביריניות`, reporting an unpointed extra yod and BHL/body versus appendix disagreement. These remain published apparatus reports, not independent BHL consultation.

Own observation in [folio 359A](https://manuscripts.sefaria.org/leningrad-color/BIB_LENCDX_F359A.jpg): the central-column context contains `בנה בהר יהודה ובחרשים` immediately before, the target between `בנה` and `ומגדלים`, and `והוא נלחם עם מלך בני` immediately after. The word shows a separate short consonantal stroke between resh and nun, distinct from the first yod after bet and the later yod before vav. Its position supports the third-yod spelling; it is not a point below a letter. The counter-risk is overinterpreting a short stroke or inherited expectation, but the visible separation is clearer than in Samuel. No new vowel or syllable is inferred from the extra letter.

Current POB's “fortresses and towers” is not contradicted by this spelling difference. It does not identify a different class of building or warrant an English substitution.

### Amos 7:2 — defective spelling without vav

The [publisher's entry `2022.12.12-3`](https://www.tanach.us/Changes/2023.04.01%20-%20Changes/2023.04.01%20-%20Changes.html#2022.12.12-3) changes `לאכול` to `לאכל`, with holam on kaf. Its displayed Hebrew and action agree, while its Unicode prose accidentally repeats vav on both sides. That prose defect does not become a contrary manuscript reading.

Own observation in [folio 311A](https://manuscripts.sefaria.org/leningrad-color/BIB_LENCDX_F311A.jpg): the surrounding sequence is `אחר גזי המלך והיה אם`, then `כלה לאכל את עשב`, followed by `הארץ ואמר אדני יהוה`. The word has lamed–alef–kaf–lamed, with the elevated holam mark but no separate consonantal vav between kaf and final lamed. The interletter region is legible rather than an obvious lacuna. The strongest counter-risk would be a very faint or conflated vav; the native crop gives no positive support for one. This favors the publisher's defective spelling without claiming a new pronunciation.

Current POB's “finished eating” remains appropriate to either spelling under the compared pointing. No English change follows from this orthographic contrast alone.
