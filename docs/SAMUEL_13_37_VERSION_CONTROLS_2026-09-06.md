# 2 Samuel 13:37: Greek, Latin and Syriac controls — 2026-09-06

## Result

The consulted Greek and Syriac editions have a d-ending patronymic, but the
Vulgate has **Amiur**, ending in r. The Cambridge apparatus additionally reports
Old Latin **Amiot**, not the Vulgate spelling. These are distinct textual
controls, not a unanimous ancient-version vote for one Hebrew form. The
[Hebrew controls](SAMUEL_13_37_HEBREW_CONTROLS_2026-09-06.md) and
[earlier Leningrad image check](SAMUEL_EZEKIEL_CODEX_IMAGE_FOLLOWUP_2026-09-06.md)
address different evidence layers. No source, English, note, review status or
export was changed by this pass.

The [machine record](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/samuel13_37_version_controls.v1.json)
pins the canonical baseline, consulted PDFs, locators, short observations,
access failures and uncompleted gates. Modern edition pages were consulted on

## Cambridge apparatus: actually read, with its conventions

[Brooke, McLean and Thackeray, volume II.1 (1927)](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_2-1.pdf),
printed pp.149–150 / PDF pages 165–166, was rendered and visually inspected,
together with Samuel's preface v–viii / PDF 11–14. The general
[1906 Genesis preface](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-1.pdf),
printed i–v / PDF 9–13, was also read and visually checked. The outer-margin
**B** identifies the Vaticanus-based printed text at this locus. It is not
an eclectic reconstruction certified as the earliest Greek wording.

The local name unit reports the following forms. Greek accents are omitted
in this analytical transcription; Latin spellings and witness scopes are
preserved. The following table does not reproduce the entire verse apparatus.

| Form | Exact local authority / edition-level interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `εμιουδ` | Printed base lemma |
| `Emiuda` | Coptic superscript w: Morgan M567 edition/facsimile report |
| `εμιουθ` | Angular-bracketed 236, 242: readings quoted on Holmes–Parsons authority |
| `εμμιουδ` | p = 106, Ferrara, Biblioteca Comunale 187 i, ii and 188 ii |
| `αμιουδ` | **b n o c₂ e₂**, plus Ethiopic superscript s |
| `Amiot` | Old Latin superscript b: Belsheim's 1885 Palimpsestus Vindobonensis |
| `Iemiul` | Coptic superscript c: Ciasca's published Sahidic fragments |
| `θωουδ` | N |

The upright **b** is an editorial aggregate of b′ (19, Rome Chigi R.vi.38)
and italic b (108, Vatican Greek 330), not a single manuscript 108 vote.
Here n is 119 (Paris Greek 7), o is 82 (Coislin 3), c₂ is 127 (Moscow Synodal
Greek 31), and e₂ is 93 (London Royal 1 D ii). Samuel's own mappings control;
one must not import sigla identities from another volume.

Ethiopic superscript s is specifically Dillmann's **S**, not the entire
Ethiopic tradition: the Samuel editors restrict their use to S and A, with an
unqualified symbol indicating agreement. The Coptic and Old Latin forms are
the Cambridge editors' published reports, here printed in Latin characters;
we did not inspect the underlying manuscripts or original Coptic/Ethiopic
letters. The 1927 preface's description of Jacob of Edessa concerns a
Greek-influenced Syriac revision, not interchangeable Peshitta evidence.

The mourning apparatus begins at the bottom of p.149 and continues at the top
of p.150. King placement, omission of David, singular/plural son(s), and
duration have separate units. In particular, the plural-sons support **b o c₂
e₂** is not the patronymic group **b n o c₂ e₂**. The David-omission unit is
separate again. Gothic Armenian A and Greek Alexandrinus A must not be merged.
We do not claim that every Greek witness explicitly names David. The general
preface also warns against treating an unlisted version as positive support.

An independent agent read the actual pages and native apparatus crop with the
question and preliminary interpretations available. It corrected the initial
working omission of n from the patronymic group and clarified Old Latin final
t in **Amiot**. This was a report-aware apparatus check, not a blind experiment,
human specialist endorsement, or formal review of this final report. The later
review receipt, if present, has its own exact scope and artifact hashes.

## POB consequence and next decision

The current file says **Ammihur**. Its sole note after that name explains the
implicit mourner David; it does not disclose the name variation. That is an
actionable disclosure/anchor candidate, separate from changing either source
consonants or the English name. The old cross-check score belongs to its old
review record and cannot approve a new candidate automatically.

Keep five decisions separate: Leningrad's written form; traditional qere;
earliest attainable patronymic; English transliteration; mourning referent and
note placement. The strongest contrary evidence against an automatic name
replacement is the r-ending Vulgate control together with the unresolved
Hebrew/editorial questions. Conversely, retaining Ammihur without disclosure
should not be portrayed as proving that reading historically prior.

Next: finish source-specific Hebrew/codex and critical-apparatus checks; draft
an exact, separately reviewed note/anchor candidate; decide whether any
source/name change is justified; and, only if applying an approved candidate,
invalidate stale review metadata and verify derivatives/exports. Do not import
the Greek geographical expansion or a named subject into Hebrew merely because
they appear in a consulted translation. No new decipherment, full version
census or manuscript-level historical priority has been established.

separate publisher Greek/Latin URLs worked. The ordinary in-app browser loaded
the CAL chapter and exact token successfully without bypassing access controls.
In-app tab export was unsupported: **no raw page export exists**. The JSON's
short CAL excerpts are manually recorded observations from the displayed row,
not a raw HTML archive or a complete copyrighted chapter. CAL's AI links were
not used as evidence. No full modern Greek, Latin or Syriac apparatus was
acquired in this pass.

Private PDF page renders and a native apparatus crop were made with Poppler
and Pillow; no generative editing, sharpening or letter filling occurred.
The first crop was too low to include the name and was replaced after visual
inspection; the final crop rectangle is recorded. OCR was only a locator aid:
an oversized extraction was truncated and was not treated as a complete read.

private; this report does not grant redistribution rights.
