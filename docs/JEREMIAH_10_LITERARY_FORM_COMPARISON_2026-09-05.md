# Jeremiah 10: a bounded comparison of literary forms

Checked 2026-09-05. [Machine-readable record](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/jeremiah10_literary_forms.v1.json).

**Result:** Jeremiah 10:10 has ancient Hebrew support for part of its wording,
while the consulted Greek continuous text lacks the verse. The familiar claim
that 4Q71 directly preserves the whole shorter sequence needs qualification:
its sequence is reconstructed from surviving line ends and available space.
The Greek apparatus also contains a marginal version of verse 10. This is
published-edition comparison, not newly deciphered manuscript ink. Current POB
English and order remain provisionally retained; earliest-form priority is open.

## Scope, source identity, and actual acquisition

The existing casebook queued Jeremiah 10:6–10 but had zero coverage records.
The all-39-book map lists six Jeremiah QDR records, 305 reference anchors, and
1,364 WLC anchors. Those discovery totals remain unchanged. This pass examines
10:10 within 10:3–13, with 10:5/9 alignment as necessary context; it does not
collate all Jeremiah or map the book's Oracles against the Nations.

The actual edition consulted is Emanuel Tov's Jeremiah material in *Qumran
Cave 4.X: The Prophets*, DJD XV (1997): printed pp. 158, 171–176, 203–204,
plus the transcription conventions on pp. 4–5. The downloaded
[edition scan](https://messianicessenes.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/djd-15.pdf)
is a third-party host of the primary edition; the host supplies no new
scholarly authority or redistribution permission. No PDF or full transcription
is vendored. Hashes and exact PDF/printed page pairs are in the record. PDF
pages were rendered and read visually because OCR mangles Hebrew and brackets.

DJD permits a common scroll for 4Q71/4Q72a despite probable different hands.
[Langlois's 2019 reassessment](https://michaellanglois.org/medias/langlois-2019-the-book-of-jeremiahs-redaction-history-in-light-of-its-oldest-manuscripts.pdf),
pp. 16, 18–20, instead favors the same hand and possible same scroll. It dates
both around the middle of the first century BCE, challenging DJD's first-half
second-century BCE date. These are competing published assessments, not a
completed physical identity or dating adjudication. Keep the upstream labels;
do not count them as two independent chapter-10 witnesses or silently merge them.

## What survives, and what is supplied

DJD pp. 4–5 distinguishes certain, probable (overdot), possible (overcircle),
and unidentified letters. Square brackets supply lost text. Reconstruction
can be an aid to the reader and does not certify every restored agreement.

At 4Q70 col. V part 1, fragment 11 line 2 (p. 158), the edition prints the
unbracketed core **מלך עולם מקצ**. The mem and lamed of מלך carry editorial
probability marks; the verse's beginning and continuation are bracketed.
In particular, the opening “Yahweh … true God … living God” wording in line 1
is supplied, as is the continuation after the surviving מקצ prefix in line 2.
This is positive Hebrew evidence for the *presence of verse-10 material*,
including “everlasting King,” not direct attestation of the entire current
English verse or its exact “true God” phrasing.

For 4Q71, p. 173 prints the line-6 ending תכלת וארגמן (blue and purple;
initial taw marked probable), and line 7 יאבדו מן ארעא (will perish from the
earth; initial yod marked possible). These are content anchors in 10:9 and
10:11 respectively. The latter is **Aramaic**, not Hebrew retroversion.
The main wording of 10:5 is lost. DJD pp. 174–176 constructs the intervening
sequence and minuses from line length: only about 10–15% of original line
length remains. Its reconstructed 5a–9–5b sequence and absence of 6–8/10
are a published spatial argument, not an intact omission boundary.

The pinned QDR `4Q71/f1` has unbalanced bracket syntax, already flagged by the
book map. QDR line 6 supplies the long clause before the colored fabrics;
line 7 supplies the rest before the Aramaic line ending. Extracting those
verse tags as complete preserved verses would reverse the edition's meaning.
No bracket repair or direct-word coverage percentage is introduced here.

## Greek wording, order, and the opposing marginal evidence

The directly consulted Greek control is Swete, *The Old Testament in Greek*,
III, third edition (1905), printed pp. **245–246**, PDF pages **269–270** of
the [hash-matched scan](https://archive.org/download/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft_202003/theoldtestamenti03swetuoft.pdf).
Its page base is B (Vaticanus); this printed edition is not a newly inspected
Vaticanus manuscript image. Both pages and the relevant introduction/sigla
were consulted, so the apparatus continuation is not lost at a page break.

In the continuous text, the idol-description clause labeled 5a is followed
by the silver/gold/fabrics unit labeled 9, then the carried-idols warning
labeled 5b, then the gods-will-perish statement labeled 11. The opening
“scarecrow in a cucumber field” of current POB 10:5 is not the literal wording
of Greek ἀργύριον τορευτόν (“worked/chased silver”). That difference requires
a separate translation/source-wording inquiry: cutting and moving current
POB verses would not produce a faithful translation of this Greek text.

| Unit aligned by content | Current POB/WLC | Swete B continuous text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idol-description / inability to speak (5a) | Followed immediately by the carried-idols clause | Followed by the precious-metals/fabrics unit (9) |
| Precious-metals/fabrics unit (9) | After verses 6–8 | Between 5a and 5b; internal wording/order also differ |
| Yahweh, living God and everlasting King (10) | Included after 9 | No corresponding continuous-text unit |
| Gods will perish (11) | Follows 10 | Follows 5b |

Crucially, p. 246 explicitly reports a Greek verse-10 addition under **Qᵐᵍ**,
beginning `ο δε κς θς αληθινος εστι` and including `βασιλευς αιωνιος`.
The siglum identifies Marchalianus marginal evidence, distinct from its
continuous text; it must not become either Vaticanus text or uniform Old Greek
support. The preceding apparatus also carries 6–8 and a form of 9. The record
retains the verse-10 attribution separately without claiming all these units
have one physically verified hand. Greek transmission therefore includes
Hebrew-aligning longer material as well as the shorter continuous form.

The limited Hebrew line ends cannot uniquely recover the source behind every
Greek expression. Conversely, the spatial Hebrew evidence for a shorter
arrangement makes translation abbreviation alone an insufficient account of
the whole phenomenon. Literary-form and translation differences coexist.

## English effect and adjudication

Current POB 10:10 reads: “But Yahweh is the true God; he is the living God and
the everlasting King. At his wrath the earth quakes, and the nations cannot
endure his indignation.” Footnote markers are omitted in this quotation only.
Within 10:3–13, the longer form alternates idol polemic with Yahweh-focused
praise; choosing the shorter form removes this whole assertion at this point,
as well as 6–8, and moves the metals/fabrics material within verse 5.
That is a source-form choice with paragraph-level literary effects, not an
English fluency improvement or permission to erase a verse silently.

Working outcome: **provisionally retain the declared longer POB/WLC form** and
prepare disclosure of the shorter alternative. The ancient 4Q70 core is a
strong objection to calling verse 10 simply a late medieval addition.
The 4Q71 spatial reconstruction and shorter Greek text are the strongest
objection to treating POB's longer form as demonstrably earliest. Neither
“shorter is earlier” nor the age of a physical manuscript settles composition.
Langlois's dating dispute further prevents an exact chronology-based ranking.
Removing the modest preference for older objects would leave the decision held.

Draft disclosure, **not applied or independently reviewed**:

> The shorter Greek form lacks verses 6–8 and 10 and places the material of
> verse 9 within verse 5. The reconstructed arrangement of the fragmentary
> Hebrew manuscript 4Q71 supports that shorter form. Another early Hebrew
> manuscript, 4Q70, preserves part of verse 10; Greek marginal evidence also
> contains a form of it. This translation retains the longer Hebrew form.

This draft is a literary-form note, not a fresh English candidate assessment.
No blind review, new canonical text, registry entry, coverage record, frozen
receipt change, or independent manuscript count is claimed.
