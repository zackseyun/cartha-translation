## 1. ArugLev: actual edition, not an inferred name expansion

The Center's [publications page](https://jeselsohncenter-he.com/%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D/) links both Eshel, Baruchi and Porat publications:

- [Megillot 3 (2005), 259–260](https://jeselsohncenter-he.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/eshel-baruci-and-porat-2005-megilot-hebrew.pdf): initial photographs before cleaning; less text than the later edition. Both complete pages inspected.
- [DSD 13/1 (2006), 55–60](https://jeselsohncenter-he.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/eshel-baruchi-and-porat-2006-dsd-english.pdf), DOI `10.1163/156851706776205941`: all six pages, including photographs, transcription, notes and footnotes inspected. The images are scanned publication illustrations, **not newly obtained original-resolution manuscript photographs**.

The 2006 account reports Bedouin discovery in 2004, photography that August, purchase in March 2005 and transfer to Israel. A retains upper margin; joined B+C spans two columns; a fourth cluster remained indecipherable. Footnote 8 says B/C were separate at first photography and subsequently glued, possibly by a dealer. The cave attribution is reported provenance, not witnessed excavation by this project; authenticity is not independently established here. Neither the modern `X` prefix nor the acquisition date alone proves forgery. The publication discusses a Leviticus scroll, not En-Gedi Lev 1–2. [2006 edition, pp.55–58](https://jeselsohncenter-he.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/eshel-baruchi-and-porat-2006-dsd-english.pdf)

The disclosed plene `בסכות` at B+C i12 concerns spelling; it produces no new English sense. Strongest contrary explanation to treating the digital concordance as independent evidence: both projects can derive their similar layout/text from the same edition or common Abegg ancestry. It identifies the comparison target, not an extra witness or fresh ink reading. The remaining narrow task is original-image/edition comparison of A4 and ii12, and explicit source-project alias documentation before any formal registry merge.

## 2. 11Q28 / 11QpapLev: a fragment-level, not whole-label, crosswalk

The actual [publisher preview of Humbert/Fidanzio, *Khirbet Qumrân and Aïn Feshkha IV A* (2019)](https://www.vandenhoeck-ruprecht-verlage.com/downloads/productPreviewFiles/LP_978-3-525-56469-1.pdf) has 26 PDF pages. Printed pp.viii–x and **xxii** were rendered and visually read. Page xxii provides these locators; question marks below are preserved from its identifications, not silently removed:

| P1038B item | Published label / proposed passage on p.xxii | Target edition page, not inspected | Current transcription |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 11QpapLv, Lev 14:25–27 | 224 | 11QpapLev frg.1:1–2 |
| 5 | 11Q28 3, 11QpapJg, Judg 12:6–7 (?) | 226 | 11Q28 frg.3:1–2 |
| 9 | 11Q28 4, Judg 13:23–24 (?) | 226 | frg.4:1–2 |
| 4 | 11Q28 5, Judg 16:4–5 | 226 | frg.5:1–2 |
| 6 | 11Q28 6, Judg 18:5 (?) | 228 | frg.6:1 |
| 7 | 11Q28 7, Judg 18:27 | 228 | frg.7:1–2, with supplied context extending into 18:28 |
| 8 | 11Q28 8, **11QpapJg (?) or 11QpapLv (?)** | 228 | frg.8:1, unassigned traces |

Thus 11Q28 is not simply another Leviticus name. Nor can all its fragments be treated as one securely identified Judges copy. The shared P1038B series does not prove all items belonged to one scroll. The preview's foreword reports recovered papyri among organic material; the body chapter needed to assess individual assignments is absent. Its table also flags a further Leviticus lead: **1032A fragment 41, Lev 13:51–52, 11Q2 (?)**, p.238. That is a future reclassification check, not a new manuscript count.

The [current 11QpapLev page](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/11QpapLev/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21), index ordinal **1083**, has only two main lines, with `Add P-1038B-3` locators. Outside square brackets it prints only `ת ומן` and `הזה`; most surrounding Lev 14:25–27 wording is supplied. This supports a precise target but not three fully surviving verses or an independent English variant. The [11Q28 page](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/11Q28/2026-05-21/index.html?v=2026-05-21), ordinal **1082**, displays units 3,4,5,1,6,7,8, totaling 12 lines. Its frg.1:1–2 proposes Judg 17:1–2 without an Add locator; that older-unit bridge remains unverified. Several other identifications rest on only a few unbracketed letters. All are published transcription observations, not our readings of ink.

There is a chronological/inventory distinction to retain. [Tigchelaar, *HTS* 72/4 (published 10 June 2016), note 9](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S0259-94222016000400006&script=sci_arttext) describes two minute 11Q28 papyri in box 988 with small 11Q1 pieces. The current [IAA B-367190 record](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-367190) says plate **988a, fragment 6**, infrared, recto, June 2013, Shai Halevi, manuscript 11Q28; its linked [manuscript page](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/11Q28-1) still says Unidentified. These are institutional metadata, not pixels inspected here. No equation of IAA fragment 6 with P1038B-6 or modern frg.6 is warranted. Exact labels 11Q28 and 11QpapLev were absent from both pinned QDR files on the bounded check; absence there does not establish missing physical manuscripts.
