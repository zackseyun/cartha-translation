# Pentateuch source comparison — pass 4

Checked: 2026-09-04

## Outcome

Deuteronomy 32:8 has been upgraded from a secondary-report pilot to a pinned
source comparison. The result strengthens—but does not yet canonically
promote—the existing working preference for a divine referent:

- 4QDeutj (4Q37) directly reads `בני אלוהים`, “sons of God.”
- The Rahlfs Old Greek control reads `ἀγγέλων θεοῦ`, “angels of God,” supporting
  the divine referent but not the identical Hebrew wording.
- The Masoretic and Samaritan controls read `בני ישראל`, “sons of Israel.”
- 4QpaleoDeutr (4Q45) overlaps the earlier portion of the verse but does not
  preserve the decisive final phrase; it is a coverage record, not a vote.

The POB main text remains aligned to its declared WLC source. Its footnote now
names 4Q37, distinguishes the Greek wording, reports the Samaritan evidence, and
states that the divine-being reading is likely earlier.

## Fouad 848 photographic-edition follow-up — 2026-09-05

The earlier secondary attribution of a complete “sons of God” phrase requires
a preservation qualification. Aly/Koenen's 1980 photographic edition, p. 120,
column 73, note 4f., prints a surviving sons-noun prefix (normalized `υιω`,
with omega marked doubtful in print), then supplies the ending and complement.
It discusses both God and Israel continuations. **848 discriminates the sons
noun from angels; these surviving letters do not decide God versus Israel.**
This is a published reading, not newly deciphered text.

The printed concordances and plate identify fragment 177, plate 46 (848),
column 73*, line 4, in the 32:7–12 range. The asterisk denotes added fragments
or another textual change relative to Dunand, not uncertain ink. Plate 46's
upper portion and target fragment were viewed after the note: this is a
physical mapping/overview, not a blind letter-level image experiment.

The same note reports the Israel continuation for `106[corr.]`, agreeing with
the earlier Cambridge report and opposing the secondary complete-phrase
attribution. Two editorial reports are not two independent manuscripts. The
precise modern Göttingen apparatus and Ferrara hand still require inspection.

[Aly/Koenen edition preview, printed p. 120 at preview 133 and plate 46 at 134](https://www.scribd.com/document/845587932/Ludwig-Koenen-Three-Rolls-of-the-Early-Septuagint-Genesis-and-Deuteronomy-a-Photographic-Edition-Papyrologische-Texte-Und-Abhandlungen-R-Habel#page=133).

## Primary Greek follow-up: preserved noun and correction report — 2026-09-05

The acquisition targets above have now yielded two bounded results. Evidence,
source-PDF hashes and exact inspection pages are recorded in the
[Greek review receipt](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/deut32_8_greek_review.v1.json).
The earlier report of 106c under “sons of God” is **not verified support**:
the primary Cambridge apparatus supplies contrary evidence below.

### Amherst 192 / Rahlfs 916

Grenfell and Hunt's 1901 edition, p. 202, CXCII fragment (a), recto line 2,
prints `] αγγε[λων` at verse 8. Its publication conventions distinguish supplied
letters in square brackets from unbracketed text. The preserved `αγγε` supports
the angel noun against `υἱῶν`, “sons”; the ending `λων` is supplied. The following
word “God” is not preserved in this quoted line. Thus this is actual published
support for one discriminating component, not a surviving complete “angels of
God” phrase and not a reconstructed Hebrew noun.
[Amherst Papyri II, p. 202 and publication conventions](https://archive.org/details/amherstpapyribei02grenuoft).

Visually inspected the complete transcription page, conventions page and plate
XXIV. The plate labels CXCII recto fragments a/b; this was an overview after
reading the edition, not a blind or complete letter-level transcription.
An initial plate-preview attempt ran before rendering finished and failed;
after the renderer completed the full plate was inspected successfully.
Kraft's reassessment proposes that related Amherst fragments belonged to an
excerpt anthology. Retain that hypothesis when assessing local textual function;
neither a common codex nor a continuous Pentateuch is established here.
[Kraft, “The Amherst Papyri Revisited,” Newground section](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/amherstpap/info/PAmhInt.html).

### Cambridge p / 106: do not combine separate apparatus units

Brooke and McLean's 1911 Deuteronomy apparatus, p. 661 (PDF 267), reports
`υιων` plus the abbreviated Israel name under the lemma `αγγελων θεου`,
including `p^b(uid)` among its cited authorities. Normalized, this is
`υιων Ισραηλ`, “sons of Israel,” with the printed uncertainty qualification
retained. It is not that edition's report of “sons of God” in this correction.
[Cambridge I.3, p. 661; preface v–vi also inspected](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-3.pdf).

The 1906 preface v–vi identifies `p` as 106 (Ferrara Gr. 187) and superscript
`b` here as a later correcting hand; preface ii requires checking the full list
of relevant variants to recover a manuscript's exact reading. These pages were
rendered and inspected, and Wevers's Deuteronomy introduction independently
confirms the p/106 crosswalk. No corrector date or manuscript pixels were checked.
[Cambridge I.1, preface ii, v–vi](https://tmcdaniel.palmerseminary.edu/Brooke%26McLean/LXX_Brooke%26McLean_1-1.pdf),
[Wevers, A.I.2, 106](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/text/religion/biblical/lxxvar/active/DT%20Intro,%20ed.%20Sigrid%20%20Peterson%20Wevers%20Introduction.html).

This conflicts with the complete-phrase attribution in the Tov table consulted
above. A plausible failure mode is treating support for the first word as
support for the whole phrase while missing a variant on the next word. That
is a hypothesis to test against the exact modern apparatus, not a demonstrated
account of how the secondary report arose. We have not inspected Göttingen
p. 347 or the Ferrara correction. Other search-result quotations of those
materials are discovery leads only. The 1911 edition predates later papyrus
publication and cannot establish the absence or reading of 848.

The formal comparison now includes Amherst's bounded noun evidence and flags
the correction-report conflict. The 26-entry registry and 20 formal physical
coverage records have not been expanded in this pass; these partial printed
checks are not a claim of comprehensive new image mapping. Main POB Hebrew,
English and notes remain unchanged. No selection or review gate was promoted.

Next: inspect both adjacent word entries in the modern Greek apparatus and
848's exact papyrus region; retain separate component-level and complete-phrase
support before resolving the existing source/English proposal.

The editor explicitly acknowledges that this exact phrase has no extant

Its proposed transmission history is an editorial hypothesis, not observed
scribal activity. [Institutional article and PDF](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/classicsfacpub/98/).

## Image status

The IAA identifies 4Q37 as a Herodian Hebrew parchment manuscript and 4Q45 as a
Hasmonean Paleo-Hebrew parchment manuscript. Both have institutional image
inventories. An exact image-region claim is not yet made for 4Q37 fragment 12.

The IAA currently labels individual image objects by plate position, while the
scholarly transcription uses DJD fragment numbers. The archive's own help page
states that correlation of the new image-fragment identifiers with DJD notation
is ongoing. Similar-looking “fragment 12” labels on different plates therefore
cannot be guessed into an evidentiary crosswalk.

## Adjudication effect

The older pilot already preferred a divine referent at moderate confidence. The
new evidence improves its basis in four ways:

1. 4Q37 is now cited through a versioned manuscript transcription rather than
   only a secondary witness table.
2. Its physical identity, script, period, material, publications, and image
   inventory are tied to the IAA record.
3. The Samaritan and Rahlfs controls are extracted from pinned datasets.
4. 4Q45 is explicitly recorded as non-support rather than silently omitted or
   counted by passage presence.

The preference remains moderate because the DJD-to-image crosswalk, image-level
verification, and individual early Greek “sons” witnesses still need review.
The evidence favors translating the critical reading as “according to the
number of the sons of God”; “angels of God” is the Greek rendering, not the
exact surviving Hebrew phrase.

## POB promotion issue

This verse exposes an important corpus-design requirement. Its YAML record says
that its canonical source is WLC, which reads “sons of Israel.” Changing only
the English to “sons of God” would make the source and translation disagree.
A future canonical promotion must therefore atomically record:

1. the selected critical Hebrew reading and its decision record;
2. the retained WLC reading and apparatus note;
3. the English rendering of the selected reading; and
4. the review state and baseline hash.

Until the application mechanism is implemented and the remaining evidence gates clear,
“sons of God” is the leading critical candidate rather than a silently adopted
English replacement.

## Atomic selection pilot

That mechanism now exists as a validated, non-promoted pilot record. It binds
the working Hebrew variation unit `בני אלוהים`, its direct 4Q37 anchor, the
candidate rendering “the sons of God,” the unchanged WLC/English baseline, the
retained Masoretic, Samaritan, and Greek readings, and each unfinished review
gate in one object.

The pilot intentionally has no full-verse critical Hebrew text. Its status is
therefore `not-promoted`, and the canonical verse remains unchanged. Validation
rejects a stale baseline, an unsupported or coverage-only source manuscript,
generated-image evidence, a source/English mismatch, or a promotion attempt
while any gate is pending. The current pilot also rejects every promotion
claim because application receipts and source/English/export verification are
not implemented. A future application format must carry those records in
addition to the approved source and English. See the
[method review](TEXTUAL_RESTORATION_APPROACH_REVIEW_2026-09-04.md).

A separate [full-record research draft](APPLICATION_DRAFT_PREFLIGHT_2026-09-05.md)
now supplies a concrete source/English/notes candidate and a real local export
probe. It is an explicitly labeled editorial composite, not an approved
full-verse source or new fragment transcription. Its creation leaves the
selection's null full-verse field and pending gates unchanged. The preflight
demonstrates schema and disclosure gaps rather than certifying publication.
