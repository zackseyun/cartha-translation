# Source-near editorial standard

This standard supplements `DOCTRINE.md` and `docs/REVISION_PROCESS.md`. It exists because a rendering can be lexically possible word by word and still be misleading, overinterpreted, or unnatural as an English verse.

## Governing order

1. **The source text controls.** Establish morphology, syntax, discourse, and poetic structure before consulting English translations.
2. **Translate what is expressed.** Do not insert an event, speaker, motive, doctrine, or explanatory adjective into the main text merely because it makes one interpretation easier.
3. **Preserve real ambiguity.** Choose the least committal natural English available. When English must choose, state the principal alternative and the reason in a footnote.
4. **Use natural English, not gloss-English.** A lexically possible gloss is not automatically a good translation. Translate ordinary source-language idioms into ordinary English and preserve the literal form in a note when it matters.
5. **Preserve images before explaining them.** Keep concrete metaphors in the verse. Put inferred effects or explanations in notes unless the source itself states them.
6. **Match register and literary shape.** Read the paragraph or poem aloud. Parallel lines should sound like one composition, not independent dictionary entries.
7. **Published translations are diagnostics, never votes.** NKJV and other licensed translations may expose unexpected divergence; public-domain panels may rank review candidates. Agreement does not prove accuracy, and novelty does not prove improvement.

## Required review questions

For every revised verse, the editor records:

- What does the source explicitly say?
- What has English grammar forced us to supply?
- What interpretation, if any, did we keep out of the main text?
- Is every uncommon English word necessary for accuracy or register?
- Does the verse read naturally aloud in its chapter?
- If POB differs materially from NKJV/NIV/NRSV/NET or a strong public-domain consensus, is the source-based reason documented?

## Automatic-review limitations

A model agreement score is evidence that the draft passed that prompt, not evidence of correctness. No verse is called source-audited merely because multiple models marked it `agree`. A source audit requires a recorded adjudication that checks the source construction, chapter flow, natural English, and meaningful comparison divergences.

## Book-level sequence

1. Rank verses by documented ambiguity, reader complaints, unusual English, and divergence from the reference panel.
2. Review complete poetic units or discourse sections, not isolated verses only.
3. Start with the highest-risk verses, then read every chapter aloud end to end.
4. Record each accepted or rejected change with source evidence.
5. Synchronize POB, SPOB base grounding, reader footnotes, and generated assets.
6. Report separately: automated review, source-audited verses, named human/scholar review, and publication state.

## Wisdom-book order

Use this order for the present correction program: Psalms, Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, then Song of Songs. Psalms begins with the current reader-reported chapter and the highest divergence/risk queue; the surrounding wisdom books must not inherit a claim of review from Psalms.
