# John 21:15–17: agape-love and phileo-love

Approved by the maintainer on 2026-09-06 for POB and SPOB.

## Decision and reasoning

Preserve the two Greek love verbs directly in the English reading text with
the hyphenated forms **agape-love** and **phileo-love**. POB's commitment to
the authority of Scripture motivates preserving its vocabulary choices rather
than leaving this distinction visible only in a footnote. This editorial
decision treats the wording as intentional and makes the pattern available
to every reader.

| Verse | Jesus' question | Peter's answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| John 21:15 | ἀγαπᾷς — agape-love | φιλῶ — phileo-love |
| John 21:16 | ἀγαπᾷς — agape-love | φιλῶ — phileo-love |
| John 21:17 | φιλεῖς — phileo-love | φιλῶ — phileo-love |

The repeated question inside the narration in verse 17 also reads
**“Do you phileo-love me?”** The third question therefore visibly uses the
same verb as Peter's answers. There are seven rendered occurrences across
the three verses, including that narrative repetition.

**Agape-love** uses the familiar English label *agape* to distinguish forms
of the Greek verb **ἀγαπάω** (*agapaō*). **Phileo-love** represents forms of
**φιλέω** (*phileō*). The Greek source text remains unchanged; these are
English editorial renderings, not changes to the source or a claim that the
noun ἀγάπη occurs in these questions.
