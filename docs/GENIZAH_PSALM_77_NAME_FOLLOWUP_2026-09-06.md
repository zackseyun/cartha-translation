# Genizah Psalm 77 divine-name follow-up — 2026-09-06

## Result

Actual images support Cambridge's report that **T-S AS 67.19 uses a divine-name abbreviation at Hebrew Psalm 77:8 where the Masoretic base has Adonai**. The target is **POB Psalm 77:7**, not POB 77:8. At the target the surviving sign has two yod-like letter bodies; a nearby YHWH slot uses a comparable sign, while a nearby Adonai slot appears as an alef/dalet abbreviation. This establishes a local written-form difference more securely than the previous catalogue-only lead.

It does **not** establish four visible consonants יהוה, the exact spelling of the exemplar, or historical priority. The working outcome is **no change** to current source/English, with priority held rather than a demonstrated victory for the Masoretic reading. No canonical, registry, central, or frozen artifact was edited. The [machine-readable record](../sources/textual_restoration/discovery/genizah_psalm77_name_followup.v1.json) binds the assets, locators, control pins and limitations.

This is context-informed agent inspection following the controlling method, not a fresh blinded transcription, human/specialist assessment or independent image-review result. The catalogue interpretation was known before the images were examined.

## Interpretation, contrary explanation and English consequence

The strongest support for Cambridge's observation is the combination of the target sign, the same-leaf YHWH-slot sign, and the different alef/dalet abbreviation at another Adonai slot. A universal claim that this scribe writes every Adonai with the same divine-name symbol is therefore poorly fitted to the inspected local evidence.

The strongest contrary explanation remains **occasional substitution or normalization while abbreviating/reciting**. A shorthand sign can represent a divine referent without copying the exemplar's consonants mechanically. Nearby אד weakens an across-the-board normalization theory but cannot exclude a single substitution. Cambridge's [interview with Kim Phillips](https://genizahfragments.lib.cam.ac.uk/2021/08/25/qa-wednesday-decoding-shorthand-bibles-with-kim-phillips/) explains how initial-word/syllable shorthand can aid recitation, while other systems serve scribal reference purposes. That is methodological context, not proof of this leaf's purpose or the cause of this particular sign. The page shows August 25, 2021 and a May 14, 2025 date; neither dates AS67.19.

Attestation of this copy's abbreviated form is materially stronger than before. **Historical priority remains held**: the catalogue supplies no copy date, related shelfmarks are not extra votes, and no full-word early witness or critical apparatus has yet been compared at this precise locus. The modest chronological preference cannot manufacture a date or overturn those gaps.

Under current project policy, selecting יהוה in a future source adjudication would change “the Lord” to **“Yahweh.”** That would be a source-wording/name decision, not an English-only modernization. This image alone does not warrant it. Retain current wording provisionally and consider a source note only through a separately reviewed disclosure/application transaction.
